# Lens Loop

A single-page web app for a phone: prop the phone up, open the page, tap **Start**. Every N seconds (15 by default) it takes one frame from the camera, sends it to Claude through the Messages API, and streams the answer onto the screen. The previous answers stay in a list below.

- `index.html` is the whole app (plus `pcm-worklet.js`, the microphone capture worklet). No build step, no server; it talks to `api.anthropic.com` and, for speech recognition, `api.elevenlabs.io` directly from the browser.
- Your API key is typed into the page's settings and stays in that phone's browser storage. Nothing in this repository holds a key.
- Model and effort come from two lines near the top of the script in `index.html` (`const MODEL` and `const EFFORT`, currently `claude-fable-5-1` at `max`) and apply on every open; edit those two lines on GitHub to change them. Other defaults: one frame every 15 s, 1568 px images, 32,024 max output tokens, large text; all adjustable in the settings sheet.
- Every reply is asked (via a fixed system prompt) to start with a line `ANSWER: …`, which the page renders as a large headline above the explanation, so a multiple-choice verdict such as `C) $2000` is readable from across the room.
- The camera fills the screen; the answer panel runs from just under the camera strip to the bottom and can be hidden with a tap to see the whole camera.
- Tilt the phone sideways and the answer panel turns to stay readable while the camera view stays exactly where it is; the captured photo is also turned upright before it is sent. Tilt comes from the motion sensor (iOS asks for permission on Start) and works best with Orientation Lock on. Both behaviours can be switched off in settings.
- On phones whose browser exposes camera zoom (iOS 17+), zoom presets appear on the view (widest lens, 1×, 2×) and the widest is used by default; every lens the phone reports is also listed in the camera picker. The camera is asked for its highest resolution before the frame is scaled to the chosen size.
- A link of the form `…/lens-loop/#key=sk-ant-…` carries the key in the fragment, which browsers never send to the server. The page reads it on every open and keeps it in the address, so a bookmark or Home Screen icon made from that link launches with the key. It is also copied into the browser's storage so the plain address works while that lasts.

## One tap, then hands-free

- **Start** asks iOS once for motion access and for the camera and microphone together (one prompt), opens the camera, starts the frame loop and starts recording a storyline. From then on nothing needs tapping: the recording runs for as long as you talk, the camera is watched twice a second and **whatever new thing appears and holds still is read at once** — a page with a written question, a note, a sketch — and answered without a word being said — from the storylines when they hold the answer, as a clearly marked suggestion consistent with the lore when they only go part of the way, and on its own merits (general knowledge, a calculation, a problem on the page) when it has nothing to do with them (a check also runs every N seconds; a picture that has not changed since the last frame sent is never re-sent, so a static desk costs nothing), and the storyline is saved, summarised and mirrored to Drive when the session ends. The Home Screen app remembers the permissions, and the Drive sign-in refreshes itself.
- Spoken commands all begin with the word **Lens**, so ordinary talk never triggers one, and the command sentences are left out of the transcript:
  - "Lens, *question*" — answers from the storylines, with the current camera frame attached (say just "Lens." and the next sentence is taken as the question);
  - "Lens, look at this" / "take a picture" / "read this" — sends the current frame with those words;
  - "Lens, new chapter" / "new storyline" — saves the current storyline and starts the next one, same microphone;
  - "Lens, stop recording" / "end session" / "that's all" — ends the session and saves;
  - the text on screen, by voice: "Lens, hold" keeps it there (nothing new is generated or shown) until "Lens, next", which also moves on at once without waiting for the quiet spell; "Lens, again" plays it from the top; "Lens, slower" / "faster" change the pace by half a second per word; "Lens, pause" / "play" stop and start the scrolling.
- **The recording is usable while it is still running.** Everything said so far in the current session goes into every request as "Current session (still being recorded)", after the cached saved storylines, so a question shown to the camera or spoken five minutes into a two-hour session is answered from those five minutes at once — no need to stop first. It is sent as frozen blocks of about 4,000 characters (an utterance never straddles two) with the cache mark on the last frozen one, then a small open tail with the newest sentences and the sentence in progress, so between one frame and the next only the tail is new and the hours already spoken are billed at the cache-read rate rather than full input price on every request — a request does not get slower or dearer as the session grows. The session's visual notes are frozen the same way after the words (the newest 60–80 of them go in).
- **A session has no time limit.** Nothing in the page stops, degrades or caps a session by the clock: the recording, the camera loop and the answers run until Stop. Past about 240k characters of live transcript (roughly five hours of nonstop talking) the oldest blocks are left out of the request — a quarter at a time, so the cache is not rebuilt every few minutes — and Claude is told how many words from how far in are missing; the recording and the draft keep every word, and the whole session is saved and summarised at Stop. A frame whose request failed is sent again on the next tick even if the picture did not move (sooner, with growing spacing, after a passing failure such as a network blip or an overloaded API), a spoken question that failed that way is asked again once, and a camera the phone took away for a call or another app is reopened by itself.
- **The answer plays as a feed, not a page.** Each camera answer opens in the reader by itself and plays word by word at one word every 1.5 s by default (a fifteen-word sentence takes about 22 s; −/+ set 0.25–4 s per word, Pause/Auto, A−/A+), laid out lyrics-style: the phrase being read is the biggest and bright and sits in the upper part of the box, the phrases already read fade above it, the ones to come wait below, smaller and dimmer, and inside the live phrase the word being read carries the accent — upright or sideways. The reply starts playing as soon as its first sentence has streamed in, while the rest arrives (the words come far faster than they are read), so the wait is the model's thinking, not its writing. A picture that already has an answer picks the feed up where it was left, and while the camera stays on the scene the text is about (a hand-held phone drifts a little; that is not a new picture) the text on screen is never replaced. The feed takes the answer panel's place over the lower part of the camera — dark and translucent, never the whole screen — so the status bar, the camera strip and the Snap/Stop buttons stay in view and in reach; Done closes it. Like the answer panel, it turns to match how the phone is tilted (the setting *Turn the answer text to match how the phone is tilted*); held sideways, it grows upward to just under the clock, over the camera strip and the top bar, so the lines run the full length of the screen.
- **Your voice leads, and nothing changes while you talk.** While you speak, the panel follows your voice instead of the clock: the highlight goes to the words you say — anywhere in the text, so you can read from the top after the timer ran ahead — and the timer picks up from there a few seconds after you stop. Because you are reading the text on screen, this is alignment rather than recognition: your last few words are laid over the text and the place agreeing on most of them wins, so the engine mishearing some of them (an accent, a soft microphone, a word cut off) does not lose your place. It runs in real time: the microphone is streamed in 100 ms pieces, the words come back from ElevenLabs a few hundred milliseconds after they are said, and between two partials the highlight keeps moving at the pace you have been reading at with a lead that covers that delay, so it sits on the word being said rather than the one already said — never more than three words past the last words heard; each partial corrects it. The header names the engine that is listening. And for as long as you are talking, plus a quiet spell of 25 s after your last word (`quietSec`), no new camera text is generated or shown: the text on screen stays put, a text that was already on its way waits, and the loop resumes by itself once you have been quiet. A question you ask ("Lens, …") is answered and shown at once. Every answer — camera frame or spoken question, camera on or off — is kept with its storyline, and the readable `.txt` on Drive carries each one in full.
- **Fable 5.1 at max effort is the basis of every answer; memory makes repeats instant.** Every new text is generated by the model named at the top of the script (`MODEL`/`EFFORT`, Fable 5.1 at `max`) reading the storylines as its knowledge base and answering from its own intelligence when they do not cover the question (it is also told the reader is waiting hands-free, so it reasons no longer than the question needs). Every text is kept with its storyline — and so in the Drive folder, and on any device that picks the storyline up from Drive — together with the picture's fingerprint: a picture seen before, or a question asked before about the same picture, gets the text it got then with no request at all. Every text carries a small bracket saying what it drew on: `[Claude API · your storylines]`, `[Claude API · this session]`, `[Claude API · general knowledge]`, `[memory · Google Drive]` or `[memory · this phone]`.
- **A document shown page by page is read as one.** Every frame of the last ten minutes rides along in the request before the current one, in order and cache-marked (so it costs little), and the model is told to read them together when the current frame continues them — the next page of the same instructions, the same sketch — and to say so ("FRAMES: continues" or "FRAMES: new" on the reply's third line). Two pages of text look alike by the numbers, as does the same page moved in the hand, so when a new frame is alike but not identical to the one on screen a small fast model (Claude Haiku) glances at both and answers SAME or DIFFERENT: a page turn is never missed, and a page that merely moved never costs another full answer or replaces the text. A frame that shows nothing worth answering — the desk, the floor, a blur — gets "nothing to read" from the model and keeps the current text on screen (it is never remembered as an answer, and the empty scene is not sent again while it stays in view). And an answer about a *different* subject waits until the text on screen has played through to its end, so the document's answer is never taken away while it is still being read; an answer that continues the same document replaces it at once.
- **Drive gets it live.** While recording, the transcript so far is written to the Drive folder every 8 seconds (the "Recording · …" `.json`/`.txt`, renamed to Claude's title when the session ends), and the audio itself is saved there in 2-minute parts as it is recorded (`<title> · part 01.m4a` …; each part is a complete playable file, kept on the phone first and uploaded as soon as it is done — parts that could not be uploaded go up at the next sync). The strip under the answer shows `Drive ✓` when the last push landed. Google's sign-in token lasts an hour. **With the Drive helper** (`drive-helper.gs`, a one-file Google Apps Script deployed in your own account that holds the OAuth client secret — the one thing a public page may never contain) the sign-in is permanent: Connect asks Google for a code with offline access, the helper redeems it for an access token plus a refresh token, and from then on the page renews the access token through the helper a few minutes before every hour is up — for ever, hands-free, in any browser, for as long as a session runs and beyond. Setup is three steps, written at the top of the script file; paste the helper's web-app URL into Settings → Drive helper and tap Connect once more. Without the helper, the page renews the token in a hidden frame wherever the browser lets Google use its cookies in a frame (desktop browsers, Android Chrome); Safari on the iPhone does not, so there the mirror pauses when the hour is up: the recording, the audio parts and the photos stay on the phone and are all uploaded at the next sign-in, which the page does by itself on the next open. Uploaded audio parts and photos keep only their record on the phone, so a long session never fills its storage.
- **What the camera is shown is kept too.** Every photo the camera analyses — a sketch, a map, a page of notes — is saved with the storyline it was matched to (the one named in the answer, or the session being recorded) as a *visual note*: the photo goes to the Drive folder as `<title> · F-0012 14.03.jpg` with Claude's description, and the note (time, what it showed, the question asked about it) becomes part of the storyline's record, listed in the index and the transcript sent with every later request, so "the map I showed you on Tuesday" is answerable.
- While recording, a strip under the answer shows the clock, the engine, the Drive state and the last words heard; tapping it shows the whole transcript. The Listen/Ask/Snap buttons still work by hand.

## Read-along (voice-tracked)

- Any answer has a **Read** button, and every storyline in the book-icon list has **Read aloud**. Either opens a full-screen view of that text in large type that follows your voice: as you read your lore out loud, the word you are on is highlighted and the view scrolls itself, so you can pace a chapter or walk around the room without touching the phone or losing your place. A+/A− size the text.
- It only ever highlights the text you opened (your own lore); it generates nothing and records nothing while reading. Voice tracking uses the same speech engine as recording; if neither is available you can still scroll and read. Open it when a session is not recording.

## Storylines (listening, memory, questions)

- **Listen** records a spoken session of any length — an hour, two, more. With an ElevenLabs API key in settings (or in the link as `&stt=…`) the audio is streamed to **ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Realtime** (chosen for the lowest independent word-error rate of any speech API that can be used straight from a phone browser; 90+ languages; 50 key terms per session, which the page fills first with your own glossary — Settings → *Words to listen for*: invented names, places, terms it keeps mishearing — and then with the names and invented words from your saved storylines, so they come out spelled right; the listening language is the phone's own unless another is chosen, so an accent is never taken for another language mid-session). The microphone is captured with an AudioWorklet, run through an automatic gain stage (quiet voices are lifted towards a normal level, nothing is ever clipped), resampled to 16 kHz PCM and sent over a WebSocket with the voice detector set sensitive (`vad_threshold` 0.3) so soft speech still commits; each connection uses a single-use token fetched with the key. The engine keeps itself alive: the audio engine is resumed whenever iOS suspends it (a call, an alarm, a switch away), a microphone track that ends or stays muted is reopened, the server is asked for keepalives and a connection that answers nothing for 12 s or stops draining is replaced, and a connection that returns no text for 25 s while the microphone clearly hears sound is replaced too, again and again at a growing spacing (30 s, 1, 2, 4, 5 minutes) — the engine never gives up on its own; a quiet stretch in a noisy room looks the same from the page's side, so it only ever reconnects, and the first committed sentence resets the count. The recording strip shows the level meter and any such state. Without a key, or if the key is refused mid-recording, the phone's own dictation engine takes over (iOS ends its sessions about once a minute; the page restarts them). The transcript appears live and is saved as a draft every few seconds, so a closed tab or a crash loses nothing: the recording is restored on the next open.
- Tapping **Stop** saves the session as a **storyline** in the phone's IndexedDB (hundreds of hours fit; nothing is capped). Claude writes a title, a short summary and key terms for it. Storylines are listed under the book icon: rename, read the transcript, untick one to leave it out of answers, delete, export all as a JSON file, import on another device.
- **Ask** takes one spoken question (same engine), sends it when you pause or tap Done, with a camera frame if the camera is running, and shows the answer as the usual big headline. Camera frames taken by the loop are answered the same way, so a handwritten note or sketch can be asked about the lore.
- Every request carries an index of **all** storylines (title, summary, key terms) plus the full transcripts of as many as fit the budget (`STORY_BUDGET_CHARS` near the top of the script, about 120k tokens ≈ ten hours of speech). When the library is bigger than that, a quick low-effort call reads the index first and names the storylines the question needs, so their full text is the text that goes in; the rest stay known by their summaries. Claude is told to work out which storyline the question belongs to and to name it on the second line (`STORYLINE: …`), shown as a tag under the headline. The storyline block is marked for prompt caching, so questions within a few minutes of each other (or while the camera loop runs) are billed at the cache-read rate; the cost readout counts cached tokens and the routing call.
- Listening engine, language, name boosting and the storyline options are in settings. Neither key is ever committed to this repository: both live in the phone's storage and in the `#key=…&stt=…` link.
- **Google Drive mirror**: under the book icon, *Connect Google Drive* sends the page through Google's sign-in and back (OAuth, `drive.file` scope — the page can only see files it created; the page's own address must be listed as an authorized redirect URI on the OAuth client) and keeps every storyline in a "Lens Loop" folder as one `.json` record plus one readable `.txt`. Sync runs after every change and on open; it is two-way, so a storyline recorded on another device, or restored after the phone lost its storage, comes back on its own (newer copy wins; deleting here moves the Drive copy to the trash). Google's sign-in token lasts about an hour; the page renews it by itself — through the Drive helper (refresh token, permanent) when one is set, else in a hidden frame a few minutes before it runs out where the browser allows that, else on the next open with a silent bounce through Google (`prompt=none`) — and only asks for a tap when Google wants the user to sign in again. The OAuth client ID is in the page (`GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`); it is a public identifier, not a secret.
