# Lens Loop

A single-page web app for a phone: prop the phone up, open the page, tap **Start**. Every N seconds (15 by default) it takes one frame from the camera, sends it to Claude through the Messages API, and streams the answer onto the screen. The previous answers stay in a list below.

- `index.html` is the whole app. No build step, no server; it talks to `api.anthropic.com` directly from the browser.
- Your API key is typed into the page's settings and stays in that phone's browser storage. Nothing in this repository holds a key.
- Model and effort come from two lines near the top of the script in `index.html` (`const MODEL` and `const EFFORT`, currently `claude-fable-5-1` at `max`) and apply on every open; edit those two lines on GitHub to change them. Other defaults: one frame every 15 s, 1568 px images, 32,024 max output tokens, large text; all adjustable in the settings sheet.
- Every reply is asked (via a fixed system prompt) to start with a line `ANSWER: …`, which the page renders as a large headline above the explanation, so a multiple-choice verdict such as `C) $2000` is readable from across the room.
- The camera fills the screen; the answer panel runs from just under the camera strip to the bottom and can be hidden with a tap to see the whole camera.
- Tilt the phone sideways and the answer panel turns to stay readable while the camera view stays exactly where it is; the captured photo is also turned upright before it is sent. Tilt comes from the motion sensor (iOS asks for permission on Start) and works best with Orientation Lock on. Both behaviours can be switched off in settings.
- On phones whose browser exposes camera zoom (iOS 17+), zoom presets appear on the view (widest lens, 1×, 2×) and the widest is used by default; every lens the phone reports is also listed in the camera picker. The camera is asked for its highest resolution before the frame is scaled to the chosen size.
- A link of the form `…/lens-loop/#key=sk-ant-…` carries the key in the fragment, which browsers never send to the server. The page reads it on every open and keeps it in the address, so a bookmark or Home Screen icon made from that link launches with the key. It is also copied into the browser's storage so the plain address works while that lasts.

## Storylines (listening, memory, questions)

- **Listen** records a spoken session — a brainstorming or world-building session of any length, an hour or more — using the phone's own speech recognition (iOS Safari's dictation engine; the first tap asks for the microphone, and Siri & Dictation must be on in iOS settings). The transcript appears live and is saved to the phone every few seconds, so a closed tab or a crash loses nothing: the recording is restored as a storyline on the next open. iOS ends a dictation session roughly every minute and the page starts the next one by itself; the screen stays awake while recording.
- Tapping **Stop** saves the session as a **storyline** on the phone. Claude then writes a title, a short summary and a list of key terms for it (a quick low-effort call). Storylines are listed under the book icon at the top: rename them, read the transcript, untick one to leave it out of answers, delete, or export all of them as a JSON file and import them on another device.
- **Ask** takes one spoken question. It is sent as soon as you pause (or when you tap Done), together with a camera frame if the camera is running, and the answer appears as the usual big headline. Camera frames taken by the loop are answered the same way, so a handwritten note or sketch held up to the camera can be asked about the lore.
- Every request carries the full text of all saved storylines plus an index of titles, summaries and key terms, with the instruction to work out which storyline the question belongs to and to name it on the second line (`STORYLINE: …`). The page shows that name as a tag under the headline. The storyline block is marked for prompt caching, so while the camera loop runs or questions follow within a few minutes it is billed at the cache-read rate (a tenth of the input price); the cost readout counts cached tokens correctly. If the saved transcripts ever outgrow the context budget (about 120k tokens, roughly ten hours of speech) the oldest ones are left out first — untick what you do not need.
- Listening language and the two storyline options (use them when answering, attach a camera frame to spoken questions) are in settings.
